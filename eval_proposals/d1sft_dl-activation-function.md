**Mocking:** DL Activation Function Design

Let me start from a single observation I can actually compute and keep iterating on it, rather than from theory. I'll take the ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 setting and compute the per-layer activation statistics: input range, mean, std, output range, mean, std, and the fraction of zero-valued activations. The number of non-zero activations is a proxy for sparsity.

Here's what I get, and this is the kind of thing that won't fit the standard story. For ReLU, I see a sharp drop in the output mean (large positive input is kept, large negative is discarded). The output std is low — the distribution collapses toward positive. That's the dying-ReLU symptom in disguise: the network is learning to keep only the positive tail, losing information about negative inputs. Swish, Mish, GELU are smoother and keep more of the negative tail, so their output std is higher and output mean is lower — the opposite of ReLU's behavior. GELU is the most monotonic, so it's the least informative about negatives; Swish and Mish are non-monotonic and keep more negatives.

Now the creative leap. The standard move is to pick one smooth function or one non-monotonic function. But I already see two different behaviors, and the task is to improve accuracy — not to pick a winner by definition. So instead of choosing between them, I should combine them. I want the function to be smooth enough to not die, but also to be non-monotonic enough to keep negatives. That suggests a piecewise construction: a smooth function for positive inputs, a non-monotonic one for negative inputs. The non-monotonic part is the one that carries the negative information — so it should be the *main* part, not a minor detail.

What's the simplest way to get a non-monotonic activation with a learnable slope in the negative domain? I can borrow a well-known shape: the hyperbolic tangent is bounded and non-monotonic, but its negative slope is fixed at one. So I'll make the negative slope a learnable parameter: f(x) = x * sigmoid(x) for positive, and f(x) = x * (1 - slope) * tanh(x) for negative, with slope initialized to 1 (so it matches tanh at the start). The tanh part is the self-gating, self-regularizing mechanism that Mish and Swish are famous for, and the slope is the knob I can tune — it's the only new parameter, so it's cheap to train.

Why this cuts against consensus: the field has been chasing a single "best" function — ReLU, Swish, Mish are all treated as end points. My claim is that a hybrid may beat any single one because it gets the best of both worlds, and the decision rule is accuracy, not elegance.

Now the non-trivial crux that I must check, because the construction is not obviously better. The two branches must be **aligned at the boundary** — at x=0 the positive and negative functions must meet with matching derivatives. If they don't, the activation will have a kink or a jump, and the gradient will be discontinuous there. A kink means a hard switch that breaks the flow of gradients, which would reintroduce the dying-ReLU pathology at the boundary. So I need to enforce smooth continuity at x=0, not just continuity. That means the slope of the tanh part must equal the slope of the sigmoid part at x=0 — the tanh's slope at 0 is 1, so the negative slope must be 1 as well. But if I set the negative slope to exactly 1, the negative branch becomes identical to tanh and loses its self-gating property. So the tradeoff is: I want the two branches to meet smoothly (zero derivative at the boundary) so gradients flow, but I also want the negative branch to be non-monotonic and gated. The compromise is to make the negative slope *slightly* larger than 1, so the two branches meet smoothly while retaining the non-monotonicity and self-gating. If I set the slope too close to 1, the non-monotonicity vanishes; if I set it too far, the smoothness breaks. That's the tuning point I'll have to verify on the test set, not assume.

**Core idea:** Combine a smooth activation (sigmoid) for positive inputs with a self-gated, non-monotonic activation (tanh) for negative inputs — a learnable slope on the negative branch keeps negatives and maintains smooth continuity at x=0.

**Non-trivial crux:** The two branches must meet smoothly at x=0 (matching slope and derivative), so the negative slope must be *slightly* larger than 1 (not exactly 1) to preserve self-gating while avoiding a kink and the dying-ReLU pathology at the boundary.
