Let me not start from 'what loss makes the attack weaker.' I'll start from the physical source of membership inference and ask what mechanism actually *causes* the leakage. The attack is a decision rule: classify a sample as train if its predicted confidence is higher than some threshold. So the leakage lives in the *distribution of confidence* on train vs non-train. That reframing is the whole game — it tells me what to regularize, not just 'reduce overconfidence.'

Now the deductive step: if the leak is the confidence gap, then the fix is a *distributional* regularizer, not a pointwise one. A pointwise penalty like entropy penalizes high-confidence predictions, but it can't *equalize* the two tail distributions. Equalizing the tail distribution of logit confidence (and of loss, since loss is another confidence-like quantity) is what will shrink the gap a decision rule actually reads. I'm not being vague here — the attack uses *loss statistics*, not just logits. So I need to regularize both the *distribution of logits* and the *distribution of loss* to be close between train and non-train.

Why is this a clean, non-trivial crux? First, the distributions have to be matched across *both* train and non-train samples — a single-class KL or a single-class MMD won't do it; I need a *bilateral* divergence that pulls each tail distribution toward the other side's. Second, I have to make it *invariant* to the batch's own distribution — the loss should measure the gap between train and non-train, not the batch's internal variance. That means the regularization can't be a plain KL of the batch's logits against something; it has to compare train and non-train *simultaneously*. And finally, the regularizer's weight should be a function of *epoch* — stronger early, milder late — because early learning is more confident and leakier; I'd decay it to zero toward the end of training. I should keep the weight small enough not to hurt predictive accuracy too much — otherwise the attack wins on privacy but the model loses utility, which is the opposite of what I want. I'll also want to try it on logits and loss separately and see which one gives a better tradeoff.

Why does this cut against consensus? The field's go-to is entropy penalties and label smoothing, which are single-point regularizers. My claim is that they can't equalize the *distributional* gap, so the standard tools may not close the leakage source. The decision rule is the leak, so the fix must target the distribution, not a single value.

**Core idea:** Regularize the *distribution of logit confidence* and *loss* between train and non-train samples with a bilateral divergence (e.g. a two-class KL or MMD) to equalize the tail gap that the membership attack actually reads.
**Non-trivial crux:** The divergence must be bilateral and between train/non-train samples, not a single-class KL; it must be batch-invariant (measuring train vs non-train gap, not batch variance); and its epoch-decaying weight must be small enough not to hurt predictive accuracy.
