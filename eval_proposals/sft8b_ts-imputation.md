Every variable is a time series, so they share a common temporal structure. The variable-specific components should be small, cheap, and mostly trainable; the heavy lifting should be shared across all variables. That's the intuition: the variable-specific parts act as cheap, trainable *induction biases*, and the shared parts carry the bulk of the representation power.

Why does this cut against the consensus? The field has been chasing variable-specific encoders, and I suspect that's exactly the part that gets expensive and overfit. Shared encoders are the opposite direction: they're more likely to be data-agnostic and easier to train, especially when the number of variables is large. That's the bet I want to place.

The shared encoder needs to be *separable* so I can actually train it — I can't share a variable-specific projection if that projection is entangled with the shared weights.
